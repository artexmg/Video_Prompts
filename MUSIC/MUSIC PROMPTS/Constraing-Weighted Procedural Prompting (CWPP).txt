CONSTRAINT-WEIGHTED PROCEDURAL PROMPTING (CWPP)
==============================================

OVERVIEW
--------
Constraint-Weighted Procedural Prompting (CWPP) is a prompting strategy designed for generative video and audio models (e.g., Sora) that behave more like simulators than text generators.

Instead of emphasizing word frequency or descriptive richness, CWPP prioritizes:
- hard constraints
- procedural rules
- temporal choreography
- causal motion
- system-level behavior

The prompt is treated as an executable specification rather than a description.

The goal is not to “describe what something looks like,” but to
define how a scene, system, or performance MUST behave over time.

CWPP assumes that models respond more strongly to rules, negations,
and causality than to stylistic adjectives.

---

CORE PRINCIPLES
---------------

1. CONSTRAINTS HAVE MORE WEIGHT THAN WORDS
------------------------------------------
Hard constraints dominate output behavior.

Examples:
- NO static shots
- EVERY scene < 2 seconds
- Camera ALWAYS moving
- Nothing floats unless actively forced

Constraints override descriptive language.
Negations and absolutes ("NO", "ALWAYS", "NEVER") carry maximum weight.

---

2. PROCEDURE OVER DESCRIPTION
-----------------------------
CWPP defines WHAT HAPPENS and HOW IT UNFOLDS,
not just how things appear.

Instead of:
- “A dramatic battle scene”

Use:
- “Subjects advance, collide, recoil, re-engage; camera lags after impact; debris travels laterally under force.”

The model is guided step-by-step through a process.

---

3. TEMPORAL STRUCTURE IS EXPLICIT
--------------------------------
Time is not implicit.

CWPP explicitly defines:
- duration
- shot length limits
- sequencing
- escalation or denial of escalation

Examples:
- Duration: 25s
- Max shot length: 1.6s
- Average shot: 0.7–1.0s
- No slow buildup
- No long outro drift

This prevents the model from defaulting to slow, cinematic pacing.

---

4. CAUSALITY > AESTHETICS
------------------------
CWPP prioritizes cause-and-effect logic.

Examples:
- Impacts cause camera shake
- Energy overload causes lens failure
- Mass causes inertia
- Pressure creates shockwaves

Visuals must obey implied physics.

Aesthetic terms are secondary and must obey causal rules.

---

5. CAMERA IS AN ACTIVE AGENT
---------------------------
The camera is treated as a physical participant, not an observer.

Camera rules often include:
- dolly push / pull
- lateral tracking
- whip correction after impact
- vibration under force
- lens breathing
- snap focus during motion

“No static camera” is a foundational CWPP rule.

---

6. MOTION IS PURPOSEFUL
----------------------
Nothing drifts without intent.

Movement must be:
- directional
- force-driven
- reactive

Examples:
- forward propulsion
- evasive maneuvers
- collision response
- recoil and recovery

Ambient motion (floating, swaying, idle loops) is explicitly discouraged unless intentional.

---

7. AUDIO IS STRUCTURALLY COUPLED TO MOTION
------------------------------------------
Sound is not decorative.

CWPP binds audio to events:
- drum hits = scene cuts
- bass impacts = collisions
- tempo governs edit density
- silence used as structural punctuation

Music, SFX, and motion evolve together.

---

8. STYLE IS A MODULATOR, NOT A DRIVER
------------------------------------
Style, color, and aesthetic language are applied AFTER constraints.

CWPP treats style as a shader, not a rule engine.

Examples:
- pointillist particles
- ink-wash rendering
- analog film grain

These must obey:
- motion rules
- physics rules
- camera rules

Style cannot override behavior.

---

9. NEGATIVE SPACE IS DEFINED EXPLICITLY
--------------------------------------
CWPP actively defines what must NOT happen.

Examples:
- NO slow awakening
- NO standing poses
- NO floating particles
- NO triumphant resolution

This prevents default model tropes from taking over.

---

10. WEIGHTING IS HIERARCHICAL
-----------------------------
CWPP assumes an implicit hierarchy of influence:

1. Hard constraints / negations
2. Temporal limits
3. Causal rules
4. Camera behavior
5. Motion logic
6. Audio synchronization
7. Structural patterns
8. Stylistic descriptors
9. Adjectives

Lower layers must obey higher layers.

---

HOW CWPP DIFFERS FROM TF-IDF PROMPTING
--------------------------------------
TF-IDF Prompting:
- Weights words by frequency and distinctiveness
- Treats prompts as bags of terms
- Ignores time and causality
- Encourages descriptive density

CWPP:
- Weights rules and constraints
- Treats prompts as procedural specifications
- Explicitly encodes time, motion, and cause-effect
- Encourages behavioral clarity over verbosity

TF-IDF selects vocabulary.
CWPP controls outcomes.

---

MENTAL MODEL
------------
CWPP treats the model as:
- a physics sandbox
- a timeline engine
- a cinematography simulator

The prompt is not a caption.
It is a control document.

---

WHEN TO USE CWPP
----------------
CWPP is ideal when:
- motion matters
- pacing matters
- physics consistency matters
- cinematic energy is required
- default “pretty but static” outputs must be avoided

It is especially effective for:
- hyper-kinetic video
- music-synced visuals
- action, ritual, or transformation scenes
- experimental montage
- generative cinematography

---

SUMMARY
-------
Constraint-Weighted Procedural Prompting replaces descriptive prompting
with rule-based control.

It prioritizes:
- constraints over adjectives
- procedure over prose
- causality over vibes
- time over texture

The result is output that behaves, not just looks.
