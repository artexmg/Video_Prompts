Reference Music Prompt — High-Velocity Joy Anthem

A hyper-kinetic late-1970s glam-rock piano anthem at 155–160 BPM, locked in bright major-key tonality with constant forward momentum, built around relentless, percussive piano octaves that function like a rhythm guitar, driving the entire song with nonstop motion. The groove is straight 4/4, urgent and propulsive, with drums pushing aggressively on the kick and snare, minimal swing, and fills that feel like acceleration rather than decoration.

Harmony is simple, diatonic, and euphoric, designed for lift rather than tension; chord changes arrive fast and confidently, never lingering. The bass mirrors the piano tightly, creating a feeling of being pulled forward by pure inertia. Electric guitars appear as bright, crunchy accents—short rhythmic stabs and celebratory flourishes—never dominating the mix.

Lead vocals are exuberant, theatrical, and fearless, leaping across registers with precision and joy, delivered as a breathless monologue of self-belief, speed, pleasure, and unstoppable momentum. Lyrics are first-person, present-tense, packed with kinetic verbs and playful exaggeration, celebrating motion, power, and confidence with almost cartoonish intensity. Backing vocals act as stacked, triumphant shouts, reinforcing hooks rather than harmonizing delicately.

Production is clean, punchy, and analog-warm, with everything mixed upfront—no haze, no distance—so the track feels like it’s happening right now. Arrangement never relaxes: no breakdowns, no introspection, no darkness—only escalation, sparkle, and velocity. The song feels like running downhill with no brakes, pure joy translated directly into sound, engineered to make stillness impossible.
