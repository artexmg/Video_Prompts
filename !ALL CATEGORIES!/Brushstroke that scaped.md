SCENE BREAKDOWN (25s TOTAL)
Scene 1 — The Stroke (0–4s)

Extreme macro shot of a single thick brushstroke filling the entire frame.
Camera glides slowly across ridges of paint.
You can see striations, compressed edges, palette-knife marks.
The paint subtly breathes — not alive, but under tension.

Scene 2 — The Reveal (4–8s)

Camera continues its glide, slowly rotating.
The brushstroke begins to curve.
Negative space forms beneath it.
Without cutting, the viewer realizes the stroke is not abstract —
it is the spine of a whale, still indistinguishable as a whole.

Scene 3 — Recognition (8–13s)

Camera pulls back just enough to reveal the whale’s full silhouette.
The whale is entirely made of paint strokes, layered and sculpted.
It does not swim — it pushes against the canvas, displacing paint waves.
Paint ridges compress under its mass and rebound behind it.

Scene 4 — Escape (13–19s)

The whale surges forward.
Paint waves peel away like thick sheets being dragged by force.
The environment transitions from flat painterly abstraction
into a fully dimensional paint ocean.
The whale has escaped the painting —
now moving through depth, still made of strokes.

Scene 5 — Assertion (19–23s)

Wide cinematic shot.
The whale breaches slightly — not into air, but into layered color space.
Paint foam erupts as sculpted curls and ribbons.
Everything feels heavy, intentional, inevitable.

Scene 6 — End Card (23–25s)

Motion slows.
The whale passes out of frame.
The paint settles.
Minimal text appears, embossed into the paint itself:
“SOVEREIGN OF THE SEA”
Cut to black.
