## Character functions

Mina is the emotional center. Her problem is not simply “leaving.” Her problem is that she thinks transformation requires disappearance.

Samantha is the mask of happiness. Her fear is: “If I stop entertaining people, I disappear.”

Becca is the mask of control. Her fear is: “If I need people, they can hurt me.”

Older Mina is not there to give answers. She is there to prove that the future is listening.

## Visual rules for the whole film

Keep the apartment warm and dark: amber candles, green plants, old wood, soft bokeh, blue night outside the windows.

Keep the visions slightly colder, cleaner, emptier, and more symmetrical.

Use lemons as transition devices: lemon covers lens, yellow blur, cut to vision. Lemon juice drip, cut to rain. Candle flame, cut to train light. Reflection in cabinet, cut to future room.

The film should start handheld and social, then become slower and more centered as the ritual takes over. By the end, the camera should feel calm, almost ceremonial.

## Best final structure for DaVinci

Even though each Seedance clip is 15 seconds, don’t lock yourself into 15-second scenes. For the final cut, I’d use many clips like this:

Party clips: 4–8 seconds each.
Vision clips: 2–5 seconds each.
Confession clips: 8–12 seconds each.
Older Mina reveal: let it breathe, maybe 12–15 seconds.
Morning ending: 20–30 seconds total, slower and cleaner.

That will make it feel edited, not assembled.

The trick is to keep the supernatural simple: **the lemons don’t predict the future; they expose the fear that creates it.** That’s the line that holds the whole film together.
