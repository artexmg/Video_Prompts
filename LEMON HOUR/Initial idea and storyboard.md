TITLE: The Lemon Hour.

At midnight, three friends gather in an old apartment full of plants, candles, and bowls of lemons. It is supposed to be a silly farewell party for Mina, the quiet one with the lemon-print top, who is leaving the city the next morning.

They laugh, drink, dance, and film each other holding lemon halves over their eyes like little masks. One of them jokes, “They say if you look through a lemon after midnight, you see the truth.”

Mina does it first.

At first, nothing happens. She smiles for the camera. But then the room changes. Through the lemon, she sees the same apartment years later: empty, dusty, abandoned. The plants are dead. The lights are out. On the table sits one fresh lemon, impossible and bright.

She laughs it off, but the others try too.

One friend sees herself older, alone, watching this same video on a cracked phone. Another sees the three of them fighting in silence, no longer speaking. The third sees something stranger: a fourth girl standing with them in the room, smiling as if she has always been there.

The party mood shifts. The game becomes a ritual.

They turn off all the lights except the candles. They place the lemons in a circle on the table. Every time someone looks through one, they see a different possible future — some beautiful, some painful, some absurd. Marriage. Betrayal. Fame. Death. A baby. An empty train station. A burning kitchen. A field of lemon trees under a black sky.

Mina realizes the lemons are not showing destiny. They are showing what each girl is afraid to admit.

So she proposes one last game: instead of looking through the lemons, they squeeze them into a glass. One by one, each friend says the thing she has been avoiding.

“I’m scared you’ll forget us.”

“I’m tired of being the funny one.”

“I don’t want to be left behind.”

“I don’t actually want to leave.”

They drink the bitter lemon juice together, laughing and crying because it tastes terrible.

Then the candles blow out by themselves.

For one second, the room is completely dark.

When the lights come back, the fourth girl from the vision is sitting at the table. She looks exactly like Mina — but older, calmer, with a lemon in her hand.

She says softly, “Good. You changed it.”

Cut to morning.

The apartment is bright. The friends are asleep on the floor, surrounded by lemons. Mina’s suitcase is still packed by the door. Her phone alarm rings.

She wakes up, looks at the ticket, then looks at her friends.

She smiles.

Final shot: she picks up one lemon from the table, holds it to her eye, and looks directly into the camera.

For a moment, we see what she sees:

Not the future.

A choice.


SCRIPT IN 5 ACTS

Act 1 — The Party / Normal World
Clips 1–4 / 0:00–1:00
Warm apartment, friends arriving, lemons everywhere, Mina’s farewell party. Playful, intimate, handheld. Nothing supernatural yet, but the room should feel slightly too beautiful, too candlelit, almost like memory.

Act 2 — The Game Begins
Clips 5–8 / 1:00–2:00
They joke about the lemon ritual. First lemon-eye shots. At first it is funny. Then one girl sees a tiny impossible detail: the same room empty, a dead plant, a candle burning with no flame, a fourth shadow in the glass.

Act 3 — The Visions
Clips 9–13 / 2:00–3:15
Each girl sees a different possible future. This is where you can expand beyond the apartment: empty train station, rainy street, old apartment, a future party where nobody speaks, Mina alone in a taxi, a lemon tree under black sky. This keeps the 5-minute version from feeling trapped in one room.

Act 4 — The Confession Ritual
Clips 14–17 / 3:15–4:15
They realize the lemons are not showing the future, but their fears. The tone becomes quiet and emotional. They squeeze lemons into a glass and each girl says one truth. The room darkens. The candles go out one by one. This should be slower, more intimate, but still visually alive.

Act 5 — Older Mina / The Choice
Clips 18–20 / 4:15–5:00
Older Mina appears. Not horror — more like time folded in on itself. She says, “Good. You changed it.” Morning comes. Mina wakes up, suitcase by the door, friends asleep around her. She holds one lemon to her eye. Final shot: she looks into camera. Cut before explaining too much.

For 5 minutes, I would make the first minute charming, the second minute weird, the third minute visually expansive, the fourth minute emotional, and the final minute mythic.

The key is this: don’t make all 20 clips about the same room. Use the apartment as the anchor, but let the lemon visions give you cinematic range. That’s where the 5-minute version earns its length.
