# ACT I — THE APARTMENT BEFORE MIDNIGHT

## Revised Beats 1–4

**Act duration:** 0:00–1:00
**Core purpose:** Establish Mina, the apartment, the fact that she is leaving, the emotional weight of the place, and the warmth of her friendship with Becca and Samantha before the supernatural story begins.

The supernatural should not dominate Act I. The first act should feel like a real farewell night: intimate, warm, slightly melancholy, then alive once the friends arrive. The lemons enter as a strange detail, but the party should still feel stronger than the omen.

---

## Beat 1 — Mina Comes Home

**Time:** 0:00–0:15
**Location tags:** @exterior, @stairwell, @living_room
**Characters:** @Mina

Night. A city street outside an old apartment building. Cars pass. A few people walk by. The world feels normal, alive, indifferent. The camera moves with the street for a moment, then finds the apartment building.

One window glows warmly above the street.

Inside that window, we see the vague silhouette of a woman moving. We assume it is @Mina.

Cut to @Mina entering the building and moving through the stairwell with a bag in her hands. She is coming home, but the moment already feels like a goodbye. She enters the apartment, leaves her keys on a small table, drops her jacket on a chair, and turns on small lamps or lights a few candles.

The apartment is warm: plants, old wood, soft shadows, quiet furniture, lived-in details. It should feel like a place with history, not a set.

@Mina is alone. She is not crying, but she is not casual either. She moves like someone trying to stay busy so she does not have to feel too much.

**Story function:** Establish the apartment as an emotional space before it becomes a ritual space. Establish Mina as someone arriving at the end of a chapter.

**Key emotional note:** She is not excited to leave yet. She is trying to be brave.

---

## Beat 2 — Mina Says Goodbye to the Apartment

**Time:** 0:15–0:30
**Location tags:** @bedroom, @bathtub, @kitchen, @living_room
**Characters:** @Mina

@Mina moves through several rooms of the apartment, finishing the last preparations for the farewell party.

In the bedroom, we glimpse her luggage. It is packed or half-packed, visible but not overemphasized. This quietly tells us she is leaving.

In the bathroom, she pauses near the mirror or sink. Maybe she adjusts something small, then catches her own reflection for a second. She looks tired, thoughtful, almost older.

In the kitchen, she sets glasses, moves a bottle, straightens napkins, prepares the room for her friends. She is making the apartment beautiful for one last night.

In the living room, she adjusts a chair or pillow, lights another candle, and stops. The quiet hits her.

She sees her flight ticket or travel document near the shelf or table. The house keys are nearby. She picks up the ticket briefly, looks at it, exhales, then places it back near the keys.

She looks around the apartment and says softly:

**@Mina: “I’m gonna miss this place.”**

She says it to herself, not to an audience.

**Story function:** Establish clearly that Mina is leaving without explaining too much. Let the apartment feel like something she is already mourning.

**Visual idea:** Keys and flight ticket in the same frame. One belongs to the life she is leaving; the other belongs to the life pulling her away.

**Key emotional note:** Mina is not only leaving a city. She is leaving a version of herself.

---

## Beat 3 — Becca and Samantha Arrive

**Time:** 0:30–0:45
**Location tags:** @exterior, @stairwell, @living_room
**Characters:** @Mina, @Samantha, @Becca

Cut back outside the apartment building.

@Samantha and @Becca arrive with grocery paper bags, laughing and talking too loudly for the quiet night. Their energy contrasts Mina’s solitude. They bring noise, warmth, friendship, and a little chaos.

@Samantha is bright, funny, expressive, probably already making some joke before we hear the whole sentence. She carries herself like someone who refuses to let a farewell become too sad.

@Becca is calmer, sharper, observant. She may be carrying one of the heavier grocery bags. She laughs, but with restraint. She notices things.

They enter the building and move through the stairwell, still talking. This should feel alive and handheld, like friends arriving late to a party.

Outside Mina’s door, they ring or knock. This moment should not introduce the lemon mystery yet. Let it be about friendship.

Possible small exchange:

**@Becca: “Try not to make her cry in the first five minutes.”**
**@Samantha: “No promises.”**

@Mina opens the door. Her face changes immediately. The private melancholy lifts. They hug, kiss on the cheek, laugh, talk over each other. @Samantha enters first with big energy. @Becca follows, smiling and watching Mina closely.

They bring the grocery paper bags inside and place them on the kitchen counter or table.

**Story function:** Bring the party into the apartment. Establish the friend dynamic before the lemons become important.

**Key emotional note:** Mina’s sadness does not vanish, but her friends make it lighter.

---

## Beat 4 — The Wrong Item in the Grocery Bag

**Time:** 0:45–1:00
**Location tags:** @living_room, @kitchen
**Characters:** @Mina, @Samantha, @Becca

The three friends begin unpacking the grocery bags. The mood is warm and normal: drinks, snacks, cups, laughter, teasing.

Then someone pulls out one perfect lemon wrapped in thin white paper.

The moment is odd, but not scary yet.

Then another lemon.

Then a third.

The three lemons are placed on the table. They are too perfect, too clean, too intentional. But they do not glow. They are not magical-looking. They are ordinary enough to be more disturbing.

No one remembers buying them.

@Becca notices first that something is wrong.

Possible dialogue:

**@Becca: “Did you buy these?”**
**@Samantha: “No. I thought you did.”**
**@Mina: “I didn’t.”**

At the bottom of the grocery bag, @Mina finds a folded handwritten note.

The note says:

**One for each truth.
Cut only after midnight.**

@Samantha breaks the tension immediately, treating it as a prank.

**@Samantha: “Okay. Good prank. Now I need a drink.”**

She pushes the mood back into party mode.

@Mina tries to laugh, but the handwriting unsettles her. It feels familiar, though she does not know why.

@Becca watches @Mina notice this.

The lemons and note are left on the table as the party resumes.

**Story function:** Introduce the supernatural object and the ritual rule, but do not let the omen take over yet.

**Key emotional note:** The impossible has entered the room, but the friends are still stronger than the mystery.

---

# Act I Emotional Shape

Beat 1: The outside world continues; Mina returns to the apartment.
Beat 2: Mina privately mourns the place she is leaving.
Beat 3: Becca and Samantha bring warmth, noise, and friendship.
Beat 4: The lemons appear, but the party refuses to become afraid.

By the end of Act I, the audience should understand:

Mina is leaving.
The apartment matters.
The friendship matters.
The lemons are not random.
Something has entered the night, but no one is ready to take it seriously yet.
